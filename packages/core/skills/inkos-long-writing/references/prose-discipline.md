# Prose discipline

Load this while writing or revising prose. It expands rules 1, 3, 4 and 7 of the Skill.

## Focus

- Write the focus as one sentence with no "and also": who wants what, who blocks it, what the situation becomes.
- Test each paragraph: delete it. If the reader still understands the focus, delete it.
- Three events of equal weight is the most common failure. Pick the one carrying the stake; reduce the rest to approach and aftermath.
- Texture is not focus. Bottles, registers, headstones, incense, clothing only matter when they change what someone can do next.

## Do not justify a beat

- An action sentence carries the action only. The next action or the other person's reaction supplies the reason.
- Ban the inner-argument shape: "she wanted X, so she did Y", "he could not, so he said Z". Give the conclusion as dialogue or one blunt sentence.
- Ban mechanism narration: "the moment she let go, a gap opened, and through it it entered". Write the result; the reader infers the mechanism.
- Withholding a motive is stronger than explaining it.
- If more than a third of a scene's sentences answer "why", the scene is explaining rather than happening.

## Detail economics

- Before keeping a detail, answer: is it a cost, a gain, or another person's reaction? None of the three, delete.
- A number earns its place only if someone's attitude changes because of it. Give the price, then the decision it forces.
- Never enumerate with a colon plus commas. Spread a cost across the action: hesitate, press, notice the loss afterwards.
- Never explain a rule the reader just watched happen.
- Proof of a relationship is something already done for the other person, not a claim about closeness.

## Movement

- One paragraph, one new fact. Adjacent paragraphs must not re-explain the same thing.
- Merge three or more small consecutive actions into one comma-chained sentence rather than shooting them as separate paragraphs.
- Motivation chains stay under three steps and end in an observable action.
- Chapter endings: one turn of "he never imagined" per chapter, at most.

## Static scene and expression

Reference practice, measured over 1,416 chapters of the sample work.

**Environment** never opens a scene as a gazetteer entry. It enters through a person.
- Build the shot from four parts: position or distance, material, a cause, one visible detail. Example shape: "half a zhang from the bed stands a mud wall; being old, it has opened a few thin cracks, and through them comes the mother's grumbling."
- Anchor it to someone: the eye that sees it, the person it belongs to, or the voice coming through it. A landscape with no person in it is a travel note.
- Two sentences, then movement, speech or consequence.
- Ban the exclusion definition ("no spirit veins, no wards, only a layer of grass"). Ban the negation metaphor ("the river is not water, but countless thin threads"). Both describe by denying, and read as a manual.
- Ban stat-sheet appearance ("aura suppressed to foundation stage, indistinguishable from passing cultivators"). Appearance is what a camera can catch: a round face, a wisp of moustache, brand-new satin clothes.

**Expression** is a beat marker, not a paragraph.
- Open with a four-character turn, then immediately an action or a line: "his face darkened; he turned and snapped a few words at the steward", "her expression loosened; she laughed and took her leave".
- Measured density in the sample: 目光 5.4, 神色 5.3, 脸色 4.2, 眉头 1.8, 面色 1.2, 嘴角 1.0, 眼中 1.0, 神情 1.0 per ten thousand characters. Use them at that rate, not once per paragraph.
- One clause only. Never describe the inner mechanics of a face.
- Viewpoint may fail to read a face, and say so: "the black mist hid his face, but his expression could not have been pleasant". That is better than omniscience.
